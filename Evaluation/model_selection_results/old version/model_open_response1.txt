1. Vegetables (half your plate)

2. Healthy fat
- Avocado, a drizzle of olive oil, or a handful of toasted pumpkin/sunflower seeds. Fat helps you absorb fat‑soluble vitamins and keeps you full.

3. Vitamin C to boost iron absorption
- Add fresh tomato salsa, lime juice, or orange segments on the side so the non‑heme iron in beans is better absorbed.

4. A fermented or probiotic food (optional)
- Pickled vegetables, kimchi or sauerkraut (check labels for gluten ingredients) or plain yogurt if you eat dairy — good for gut health and flavor contrast.

5. Extra micronutrients if you’re plant‑based
- If you’re vegan frequently, be mindful of vitamin B12 (supplement or fortified foods), vitamin D and long‑chain omega‑3s (consider algae oil or a supplement).

6. Flavor and texture ideas
- Make a rice-and-beans bowl: brown rice, seasoned black or pinto beans, roasted sweet potato, sautéed greens, avocado, salsa, cilantro and a squeeze of lime.

Gluten allergy considerations
- Most plain rice and beans are naturally gluten‑free, but watch for cross‑contamination and for sauces/seasonings that may contain wheat (many soy sauces, some spice blends, bouillon, etc.). Use tamari (gluten‑free tamari) or coconut aminos instead of regular soy sauce.  
- Beer: many conventional beers are brewed with barley or wheat and contain gluten. If you have a true gluten allergy or celiac disease, avoid “gluten‑removed” beers (they may not be safe) and choose beers labeled and certified gluten‑free (made from sorghum, millet, rice, corn, buckwheat, etc.), or switch to wine, cider, or a gluten‑free sparkling beverage. Always read labels and ask at restaurants.

Practical plate rule
- Aim for roughly half vegetables, one quarter beans (protein), one quarter rice (grain), plus a source of healthy fat. This keeps the meal balanced and nutrient‑dense.
