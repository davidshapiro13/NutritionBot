# Food Left Out at Room Temperature — Safety Rules
# Sources: USDA FSIS Danger Zone, FDA, CDC
# Last updated: 2026-04-02

================================================================================
THE CORE RULE
================================================================================

Never leave perishable food out at room temperature for more than 2 HOURS.
If the room or outdoor temperature is above 90°F (32°C), the limit is 1 HOUR.

When in doubt, throw it out. You cannot tell if food is unsafe by looking at it,
smelling it, or tasting it. Bacteria that cause illness often produce no detectable
change in appearance, odor, or flavor.

================================================================================
THE DANGER ZONE: 40°F to 140°F (4°C to 60°C)
================================================================================

Within this temperature range, bacteria that cause food poisoning double in number
approximately every 20 minutes.

- Below 40°F: bacterial growth slows significantly (refrigerator)
- Above 140°F: most bacteria are killed (hot holding)
- 40°F–140°F: DANGER ZONE — food is at risk

The 2-hour rule applies to the total time food spends in the danger zone,
not just a single stretch. If food was out for 1 hour, put away, and then
left out again for another hour, it has reached its limit.

================================================================================
SPECIFIC SITUATIONS & RULES
================================================================================

--- COOKED FOOD LEFT OUT ---
Cooked meat, poultry, fish, eggs, casseroles, soups, stews, pasta, rice:
→ Safe up to 2 hours at room temperature
→ Discard if out more than 2 hours (1 hour if above 90°F)
→ Reheating does NOT make food safe if it was out too long — toxins from
  Staph aureus and Bacillus cereus survive cooking

--- BUFFETS, PARTIES & POTLUCKS ---
Food sitting on a buffet table is subject to the 2-hour rule.
Rules for keeping food safe at gatherings:
- Hot food: keep at 140°F or above (chafing dishes, slow cookers, warming trays)
- Cold food: keep at 40°F or below (on ice or in coolers)
- Rotate food in small batches rather than putting out large quantities at once
- Write the time food was set out — do not rely on memory
- Discard any food that has been sitting out for 2 hours, even if it looks fine

--- PICNICS & OUTDOOR EVENTS ---
Outside temperature above 90°F: the limit drops to 1 HOUR.
Keep coolers at 40°F — use plenty of ice or ice packs.
Store coolers in the shade, not in car trunks.
Keep raw meat cooler separate from the food cooler.
Do not open coolers repeatedly — each opening raises the temperature.

--- RAW MEAT, POULTRY & SEAFOOD LEFT OUT ---
These are the highest-risk foods. Treat with extra caution:
→ Never defrost at room temperature — even a short time creates risk
→ Never marinate at room temperature — always marinate in the refrigerator
→ If raw meat was accidentally left out more than 2 hours, discard it

--- EGGS LEFT OUT ---
Shell eggs left at room temperature:
→ Safe for up to 2 hours
→ In the US, eggs are washed and refrigerated — they must be kept cold
→ European eggs (unwashed) can be stored at room temperature, but US
  store-bought eggs cannot once refrigerated

--- DAIRY LEFT OUT ---
Milk, yogurt, soft cheese, cream: discard after 2 hours at room temperature
Hard cheese: can tolerate a bit longer (up to 4 hours) but refrigerate promptly
Butter: can be left at room temperature for 1–2 days in a covered butter dish

--- BREAD, CRACKERS, CHIPS, WHOLE FRUITS ---
These are shelf-stable and are NOT subject to the 2-hour rule.

--- CUT PRODUCE ---
Once fruits and vegetables are cut, they become perishable:
Cut melon: discard if out more than 2 hours
Cut leafy greens: discard if out more than 2 hours
Whole uncut fruit: shelf-stable at room temperature

================================================================================
POWER OUTAGES
================================================================================

If power goes out:
- Keep refrigerator and freezer doors CLOSED as much as possible
- Full refrigerator stays cold about 4 hours; half-full about 2 hours
- A full freezer stays safe about 48 hours; half-full about 24 hours
- Use a food thermometer to check temperature before eating refrigerated food

When power is restored:
- Refrigerator food at 40°F or below: SAFE
- Refrigerator food above 40°F for more than 2 hours: DISCARD
- Freezer food with ice crystals still present: safe to refreeze
- Freezer food that thawed completely: cook immediately or discard

Foods to ALWAYS discard after a prolonged outage (above 40°F for 2+ hours):
- Raw or cooked meat, poultry, seafood
- Milk, cream, yogurt, soft cheese
- Cooked pasta, rice, potatoes
- Casseroles, stews, soups
- Opened mayonnaise or tartar sauce
- Custard or pudding pies
- Cut fruits and vegetables

Foods that are SAFE after a power outage (do not need to be discarded):
- Peanut butter, jelly, jam
- Hard cheese (still in original packaging)
- Fruit juice (unopened)
- Whole fresh fruits and vegetables
- Bread, rolls, cakes, muffins
- Vinegar-based dressings and condiments
- Fruit pies

================================================================================
TAKEOUT & RESTAURANT LEFTOVERS
================================================================================

Restaurant takeout and leftovers follow the same rules as home-cooked food:
- Refrigerate within 2 hours of receiving
- Eat within 3–4 days
- Reheat to 165°F before eating

If you forgot to refrigerate takeout and it sat out overnight: DISCARD.
