# Food Safety FAQ — Question & Answer Format
# Written for NutritionBot RAG retrieval
# Based on USDA FSIS, FDA, CDC guidelines
# Last updated: 2026-04-02

This document is written in Q&A format to match how users naturally ask food
safety questions. Each answer is concise and actionable.

================================================================================
LEFTOVERS & STORAGE
================================================================================

Q: How long are leftovers good for in the fridge?
A: Most cooked leftovers are safe for 3 to 4 days in the refrigerator at 40°F or
   below. After 4 days, throw them out — even if they look and smell fine.

Q: Can I eat pizza that was left out overnight?
A: No. Pizza with cheese or meat left out for more than 2 hours should be discarded.
   Bacteria grow rapidly at room temperature, and reheating does not make it safe
   because toxins from Staph bacteria survive cooking.

Q: My soup has been in the fridge for 5 days. Is it still good?
A: No — cooked soups and stews are only safe for 3 to 4 days. Discard it.

Q: I forgot to put leftovers in the fridge right away. They sat out for about 3 hours. Are they still safe?
A: No. Perishable food is unsafe after 2 hours at room temperature (1 hour if above
   90°F). Discard the leftovers.

Q: Can I freeze food to make it last longer?
A: Yes. Frozen food is safe indefinitely, but best quality within 2–6 months for
   most cooked foods. Freeze within 3–4 days of cooking, not after.

Q: How do I reheat leftovers safely?
A: Reheat to an internal temperature of 165°F. In the microwave, cover and stir
   partway through, let stand 2 minutes, and check temperature in multiple spots.
   On the stovetop, bring soups and sauces to a rolling boil.

Q: Can I reheat the same leftovers multiple times?
A: Yes, as long as each portion is reheated to 165°F and refrigerated promptly
   between uses. But quality declines — best practice is to reheat only what you
   will eat at one time.

Q: How do I cool hot food safely before refrigerating?
A: Divide large amounts into shallow containers (2 inches deep) to help them cool
   faster. Refrigerate within 2 hours. Do not leave a big pot on the counter to
   cool overnight — that puts food in the danger zone for too long.

================================================================================
MEAT & POULTRY
================================================================================

Q: Can I thaw chicken on the counter?
A: No. Thawing at room temperature puts the outer surface in the danger zone while
   the inside is still frozen. Safe methods: refrigerator (1–2 days), cold water
   (change every 30 minutes, cook immediately), or microwave (cook immediately).

Q: How long can raw chicken stay in the fridge?
A: Raw chicken (whole or parts) is safe for 1 to 2 days in the refrigerator.

Q: How do I know when chicken is fully cooked?
A: Use a food thermometer — chicken must reach 165°F at the thickest part.
   Color is not reliable. Juices running clear is not reliable. Use a thermometer.

Q: My ground beef has been in the fridge for 3 days. Is it safe to cook?
A: No. Raw ground beef is only safe for 1 to 2 days. At 3 days, discard it.

Q: Is it safe to refreeze meat that has already been thawed?
A: It depends on how you thawed it. Meat thawed in the refrigerator can be safely
   refrozen (quality may decline). Meat thawed in cold water or microwave must be
   cooked before refreezing.

Q: The inside of my burger is still pink. Is that okay?
A: Not necessarily. Ground beef must reach 160°F to be safe — color is not a
   reliable indicator. Always use a thermometer. A pink center can still be safe if
   it reached 160°F; a brown center may not be safe if it did not.

Q: Can I marinate meat on the counter?
A: No. Always marinate in the refrigerator, no matter how short the time.
   Discard used marinade that touched raw meat. If you want to use it as a sauce,
   boil it first or set aside a separate portion before adding raw meat.

================================================================================
DANGER ZONE & FOOD LEFT OUT
================================================================================

Q: What is the "danger zone" for food?
A: The danger zone is 40°F to 140°F. Bacteria that cause food poisoning double
   roughly every 20 minutes in this range. Keep cold food below 40°F and hot
   food above 140°F.

Q: How long can food sit out at room temperature?
A: No more than 2 hours. If the room or outdoor temperature is above 90°F, the
   limit is 1 hour. After that, discard perishable food.

Q: I left dinner out on the table while we watched a movie. It's been about 2.5 hours. Can we still eat it?
A: No. Once perishable food has been out for more than 2 hours, it should be
   discarded. Reheating will not make it safe.

Q: What should I do with food at a party that's been sitting on the buffet?
A: Keep track of time. Hot food should stay above 140°F (use warming trays or
   chafing dishes). Cold food should stay on ice at 40°F or below. Discard any
   food that has been out for 2 hours.

Q: What food is safe to leave out at room temperature?
A: Shelf-stable foods only: whole fruits and vegetables (uncut), bread, crackers,
   chips, peanut butter, dry goods. Anything with meat, dairy, eggs, or cut produce
   is perishable and must follow the 2-hour rule.

================================================================================
EGGS & DAIRY
================================================================================

Q: How long do hard-boiled eggs last?
A: Hard-boiled eggs are safe for up to 1 week in the refrigerator, whether in the
   shell or peeled. Peeled hard-boiled eggs should be stored in water or in an
   airtight container.

Q: Is it safe to eat raw cookie dough?
A: No. Raw cookie dough contains two risks: raw eggs (Salmonella) and raw flour
   (E. coli). Both can cause foodborne illness. Use pasteurized eggs and
   heat-treated flour if you want to eat dough raw.

Q: My milk is 2 days past the sell-by date. Is it safe?
A: "Sell by" is a store stocking date, not a safety date. Milk can still be safe
   5–7 days after the sell-by date if it has been properly refrigerated. Smell and
   taste it — if it smells sour or tastes off, discard it.

Q: I left butter out overnight. Is it still good?
A: Salted butter can safely sit at room temperature for 1–2 days in a covered dish.
   If it smells rancid or the texture has changed, discard it.

================================================================================
PRODUCE
================================================================================

Q: Do I need to wash pre-washed bagged salad?
A: No. Rewashing pre-washed or triple-washed bagged greens can actually introduce
   contamination from your sink. Just open and eat.

Q: How long does cut fruit last?
A: Cut fruits and vegetables are perishable. Store in the refrigerator and use
   within 3–5 days. Cut melon should be eaten within 3–4 days.

Q: I found mold on my bread. Can I cut it off and eat the rest?
A: For soft, porous bread — no. Mold roots (invisible threads) spread throughout
   soft food. Discard the whole loaf. For hard cheese, you can cut 1 inch around
   and below the mold and eat the rest, using a clean knife.

================================================================================
SPECIAL GROUPS
================================================================================

Q: Are there foods pregnant women should avoid for food safety reasons?
A: Yes. Avoid: deli meats and hot dogs unless heated to 165°F (Listeria risk);
   soft cheeses made from unpasteurized milk; raw or undercooked seafood; raw eggs;
   raw sprouts; unpasteurized juice. These carry higher risk of Listeria, Salmonella,
   or other infections that can harm the baby.

Q: My child (age 3) ate some raw chicken by accident. What should I do?
A: Stay calm. Monitor for symptoms for up to 6 days: stomach cramps, diarrhea,
   vomiting, fever. If any of these develop — especially bloody diarrhea or high
   fever — see a doctor right away. Children under 5 are high-risk for serious
   complications like HUS from E. coli or severe Salmonella.

Q: I'm elderly (72 years old). Are there extra food safety precautions for me?
A: Yes. Adults 65 and older are at higher risk for serious foodborne illness.
   Avoid raw or undercooked meat, poultry, eggs, and seafood. Avoid raw sprouts
   and unpasteurized products. Reheat leftovers to 165°F. When in doubt, throw
   food out rather than risk it.

================================================================================
ILLNESS & WHAT TO DO
================================================================================

Q: I think I have food poisoning. What should I do?
A: Stay hydrated — sip water, clear broth, or Pedialyte. Rest. Avoid anti-diarrheal
   medication unless directed by a doctor (especially avoid Imodium with bloody
   diarrhea). The illness is often not from the last thing you ate — it can take
   2–3 days for symptoms to appear.

Q: When should I go to the doctor for food poisoning?
A: Go to the doctor if you have: bloody diarrhea, diarrhea lasting more than 3 days,
   fever above 102°F, inability to keep liquids down, or signs of dehydration
   (very dark urine, dizziness, dry mouth, no urination).

Q: I ate at a restaurant and got sick. What should I do?
A: See a doctor if symptoms are severe. Save any leftover food in a sealed container
   in the fridge — it can be tested. Report the illness to your local board of health
   or the Massachusetts DPH food safety program so others can be protected.

Q: How do I know if my food poisoning is serious?
A: Warning signs of serious illness: bloody stool, high fever (above 102°F), signs
   of dehydration, symptoms not improving after 3 days. Children under 5, pregnant
   women, people over 65, and anyone immunocompromised should seek care sooner.
