# Food Storage Times & Temperature Guide
# Sources: USDA FSIS, FDA, Cold Food Storage Chart
# Last updated: 2026-04-02

================================================================================
SAFE TEMPERATURES
================================================================================

Refrigerator: at or below 40°F (4°C)
Freezer: at or below 0°F (-18°C)
THE DANGER ZONE: 40°F to 140°F (4°C–60°C) — bacteria double every 20 minutes

Use a refrigerator thermometer to verify temperature. Do not rely on feel alone.

================================================================================
REFRIGERATOR STORAGE TIMES (USDA)
================================================================================

--- RAW MEAT & POULTRY ---
Ground beef, turkey, veal, pork, lamb:    1–2 days
Beef, veal, lamb, pork steaks/chops/roasts: 3–5 days
Whole chicken or turkey:                  1–2 days
Chicken or turkey parts:                  1–2 days
Variety meats (liver, kidneys, etc.):     1–2 days
Raw sausage (pork, beef, turkey):         1–2 days
Smoked sausage links or patties:          1 week
Hard salami, pepperoni (opened):          3 weeks

--- SEAFOOD ---
Raw fish (lean, e.g., cod, flounder):     1–2 days
Raw fish (fatty, e.g., salmon, tuna):     1–2 days
Raw shrimp, scallops, crayfish, squid:    1–2 days
Canned fish (opened):                     3–4 days

--- EGGS & DAIRY ---
Raw eggs in shell:                        3–5 weeks
Raw egg whites or yolks:                  2–4 days
Hard-boiled eggs (in shell):              1 week
Hard-boiled eggs (peeled):                1 week (store in water)
Milk:                                     1 week after opening
Butter:                                   1–3 months
Soft cheese (ricotta, brie, cottage):     1–2 weeks
Hard cheese (cheddar, parmesan, swiss):   3–4 weeks after opening
Shredded cheese (opened bag):             1 week
Yogurt:                                   1–2 weeks
Cream, half-and-half:                     3–4 days after opening
Sour cream:                               1–3 weeks

--- COOKED FOODS & LEFTOVERS ---
Cooked meat or poultry:                   3–4 days
Cooked fish:                              3–4 days
Cooked egg dishes:                        3–4 days
Cooked pasta:                             3–5 days
Cooked rice:                              3–4 days
Cooked vegetables:                        3–4 days
Soups and stews:                          3–4 days
Pizza:                                    3–4 days
Gravy and meat broth:                     1–2 days

--- DELI & PROCESSED MEATS ---
Unopened deli meat packages:              2 weeks
Opened deli meats (sliced):               3–5 days
Hot dogs (opened package):                1 week
Hot dogs (unopened package):              2 weeks
Bacon:                                    1 week
Cooked ham slices:                        3–4 days
Whole cooked ham:                         7 days

--- PRODUCE ---
Cut melon:                                3–4 days (must be refrigerated)
Cut leafy greens:                         3–5 days
Apples, whole:                            3–4 weeks
Berries:                                  3–6 days
Grapes:                                   1–2 weeks

FREEZER STORAGE TIMES (USDA)
================================================================================

Freezing keeps food safe indefinitely, but quality declines over time.
Best quality timeframes:

Ground beef or turkey:                    3–4 months
Beef steaks/roasts:                       6–12 months
Pork chops:                               4–6 months
Whole chicken or turkey:                  1 year
Chicken parts:                            9 months
Fish (lean):                              6–8 months
Fish (fatty):                             2–3 months
Shrimp, scallops:                         3–6 months
Cooked meat or poultry:                   2–6 months
Cooked fish:                              4–6 months
Soups and stews:                          2–3 months
Hot dogs or luncheon meat (unopened):     1–2 months
Butter:                                   6–9 months
Hard cheese (shredded):                   6 months
Egg whites or yolks (not in shell):       12 months
Bread and rolls:                          2–3 months
Cakes (baked):                            2–4 months

================================================================================
THAWING RULES (USDA)
================================================================================

NEVER thaw food at room temperature on the counter.
Three safe methods only:

1. REFRIGERATOR THAWING (best method)
   - Slow and safe; food stays at 40°F or below
   - Ground meat, poultry, seafood: 1–2 days
   - Large roasts, whole turkey: 24 hours per 4–5 lbs
   - Thawed food can be safely refrozen (quality may decline)

2. COLD WATER THAWING
   - Submerge in cold water in a leak-proof bag
   - Change water every 30 minutes
   - Cook immediately after thawing
   - DO NOT refreeze without cooking first

3. MICROWAVE THAWING
   - Use the defrost setting
   - Cook immediately after — parts may begin to cook
   - DO NOT refreeze without cooking first

Room temperature thawing: NEVER safe. Bacteria multiply rapidly on the outer
surface while the center is still frozen.

================================================================================
MARINATING RULES
================================================================================

- Always marinate in the refrigerator, never on the counter
- Discard used marinade that touched raw meat — do not reuse as sauce
- If you want marinade as a sauce, set aside a portion before adding raw meat
- Marinating time: fish up to 30 minutes, poultry and meat up to 24 hours

================================================================================
SPECIAL STORAGE TIPS
================================================================================

- Store raw meat on the BOTTOM shelf of the refrigerator in a sealed container
  to prevent juices dripping onto other foods
- Do not overpack the refrigerator — air needs to circulate to keep food cold
- Keep eggs in original carton on a middle or bottom shelf, not in the door
  (door temperature fluctuates more)
- Once canned food is opened, transfer to a glass or plastic container with lid;
  do not store in the open can
- "Best by" and "sell by" dates indicate quality, not safety — but when in doubt, throw it out
